Organized on September 28, 1911, when Slavic immigrant families drawn to the mills of the “Miracle City” gathered in a small room and resolved to build a church.

I.

Pioneers & the Yellow Brick Church

1911

A parish is organized

Eight founders meet at the Krenitzky home in Gary and collect $263. Their first priest arrives on the Feast of the Protection of the Virgin Mary, giving the parish her name.

1912
First Church

“The Yellow Brick Church”

A wooden church rises at 17th & Fillmore for $7,000, consecrated Sept 8, 1912. Its lone cupola holds a bell bought that year from the old Tolleston Fire Station for $46. In 1922 the church is raised, given two more cupolas, and clad in bright yellow brick, and the name stuck for a lifetime.

1919

A cemetery on Ridge Road

Parishioners buy seven acres on West Ridge Road and raise a brick chapel; deeded to the parish in 1927, it serves to this day.

II.

The Kedrovsky Years

1920

Incorporated in Indiana

Chartered as “St. Mary’s Russian Orthodox Greek Catholic Church of Gary, Indiana.”

1931

A parish and its priest grow together

Father Benjamin Kedrovsky publishes In God’s Field for the 20th anniversary; the parish’s choir takes first place at the 1930 & ’31 Chicagoland music festivals.

1940

The war years

272 young men of the parish serve in World War II; fourteen do not return. In 1941 more than a thousand mark the parish’s 30th anniversary with a festive ‘Russian Orthodox Day,’ and in 1951 Father Benjamin is granted the mitre.

III.

A Church of Limestone

1959
Second Church

A new home for the Golden Jubilee

Outgrowing the old church, the parish builds a church and hall of Indiana limestone at 45th Avenue for $350,000. Construction begins in 1959; the church is first served in 1960 and consecrated January 1, 1962, and the beloved 1912 bell moves with them.

IV.

The Rozdelsky Era

1960

Father Peter Rozdelsky arrives

Assigned from Akron, Ohio, he begins a rectorship of some 26 years. Granted the mitre in 1972, he serves as Dean of the Chicago Deanery and the first Chancellor of the Diocese of the Midwest, appointed by Archbishop John Garklavs.

1968
Name change

“St. Mary’s Eastern Orthodox Church”

The parishioners vote to rename the corporation, reflecting an immigrant community come of age within a new American Orthodox Church. In 1977 the parish also adopts the New Calendar.

1972
First Macedonian parish in OCA

St. Clement of Ohrid is founded

Some forty families in Gary organize St. Clement of Ohrid American-Macedonian Orthodox Church, the first Macedonian parish received into the Orthodox Church in America. Fr. Peter Rozdelsky helps Fr. Spiro Tanaskoski bring the young parish into the OCA, beginning a bond between the two communities.

Outgrowing its first church in Gary, St. Clement acquires three acres in Merrillville in 1974 and raises a building fund through festivals, bake sales, dances, and fish fries. With Fr. Spiro’s steady leadership, ground is broken in 1978, and in June 1979 the parish moves into its new church at 5444 Harrison Street. It is consecrated on August 9, 1981 by Metropolitan Theodosius and Bishop Boris of the Midwest.

1986
Name change · Diamond Jubilee

“The Protection of the Virgin Mary”

The parish takes its present name, renamed by Bishop Boris in 1985 and incorporated under it in 1986, as it marks its 75th anniversary. Weeks later, Father Peter Rozdelsky falls asleep in the Lord.

V.

Years of Transition

1987
to 1996

A decade of transition

Fathers John Zabinko, Thomas Brown, Blagoy Topuzliev, and William Bass each shepherd the parish through a season of change in a changing city, with Fr. James Doyle serving as interim administrator between rectors, until Father Peter Bodnar arrives with his family in 1996.

VI.

A New Home in Merrillville

1999

A neighbor’s welcome

While the new church is under construction, the parish is graciously hosted by the Descent of the Holy Ghost Romanian Orthodox Church in Merrillville, which opens its doors to the community.

2000
Third Church

A new home for a new millennium

Father Peter Bodnar guides the parish in buying ten acres on Grand Boulevard, just south of U.S. 30. He oversees the sale of the Glen Park church and attends the weekly construction meetings. The new church is first served January 30, 2000 and consecrated May 7, 2000, its hall and iconostasis blessed, and remains the parish’s home today.

2010
A community joins

Welcoming St. Clement, and the bell comes home

St. Clement’s, organized in 1972 as the first Macedonian parish in the Orthodox Church in America, is dissolved. Its faithful find a spiritual home at Protection of the Virgin Mary. And the beloved 1912 bell, first rung at 17th and Fillmore, comes home to rest on the parish grounds, dedicated to the founders.

2011

The Centennial

One hundred years are celebrated with the greetings of Metropolitan Jonah, remembering the parish’s exemplary pastors.

VII.

A Parish Renewed

2018

Father Jacob Van Sickle arrives

The parish’s current rector begins a pastorate that opens a season of renewal, with new converts, catechumens, and families, and a church school filling once more with children. Serving alongside him, Fr. Nicholas Denysenko has faithfully ministered to the parish as deacon and then protodeacon, and in 2025 was ordained to the holy priesthood.

2026

A monument to St. Clement

Blessed on the parish grounds in memory of the Macedonian community whose story is now woven into its own.

2026
115th anniversary

Archbishop Daniel keeps the feast

His Eminence Archbishop Daniel visits the parish on its patronal feast of the Protection of the Virgin Mary, celebrating 115 years since its founding. The community continues, well into its second century.

Under Her Protection

More than a hundred and ten years after eight pioneers gathered in a small room in a raw new steel city, the parish continues to worship, serve, and grow in Merrillville, Indiana, a parish of the Orthodox Church in America, Diocese of the Midwest.
